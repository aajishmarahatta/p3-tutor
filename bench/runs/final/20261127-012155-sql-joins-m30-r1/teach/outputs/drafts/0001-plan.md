# Plan 0001: one-to-many-joins-and-counting (O2, 30 min)

## Objective and win
- O2: explain how one-to-many relationships change the row count of a JOIN, and count records without unintended duplication.
- Win: "By the end of this lesson you can predict the row count of an INNER or LEFT JOIN on a one-to-many pair, and pick the COUNT that gives students vs enrollments."
- Mission tie: Alex wants to predict rows, see why joins duplicate, and count correctly after joining.

## Where Alex starts
- Knows: SELECT/WHERE/ORDER BY; PK/FK; INNER JOIN combines matching rows; LEFT JOIN seen but shaky (records/0001, NOTES). Use these only as recall.
- Do not assume: LEFT JOIN NULL behavior, any COUNT variant, the words grain, aggregate, NULL, fan-out, pre-aggregate.
- Known stuck points: expects one row per student; confuses students with enrollment rows.
- Do not reuse quiz 0001 items (LEFT JOIN excluded/NULL rows; INNER JOIN with one enrollment each). Every lesson example has students with 2-3 enrollments.

## Shared sample data (use throughout, small)
- students(student_id, name, tuition): 1 Ana 1000; 2 Ben 500; 3 Cy 800; 4 Dee 300.
- enrollments(enroll_id, student_id, course): Ana 3 rows, Ben 1, Cy 2, Dee 0.
- Facts: INNER = 6 rows; LEFT = 7 rows; true students = 4 (3 with enrollments); enrollments = 6.
- SUM(tuition) true = 2600 (all four); after LEFT JOIN = 3000+500+1600+300 = 5400.
- Sports analogy (one per section max, only where it fits): player and match appearances.
